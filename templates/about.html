Image Complexity Index (ICI) Methodology

Theoretical Foundations, Mathematical Modeling, and Classical Computer Vision Architecture.

1. Structural & Edge Complexity

Sharp intensity transitions dictate human spatial perception of object boundaries. High edge pixel concentration directly correlates with visual clutter.

Edge Density (D_edge) = Count(Canny_Pixels) / Total_Pixels

Contour topology counts discrete closed boundary loops and calculates total contour area ratio relative to the canvas.

2. GLCM Texture Matrix

Gray-Level Co-occurrence Matrix (GLCM) computes spatial relationships between pairs of pixel gray levels at directions 0°, 45°, 90°, 135°.

  • Contrast: Measures local variations and spatial roughness.
  • Correlation: Linear dependence of gray levels across neighboring pixels.
  • Energy: Textural uniformity (squared sum of GLCM elements).
  • Homogeneity: Closeness of GLCM elements to matrix diagonal.
3. Shannon Information Entropy

Derived from Information Theory, Shannon Entropy quantifies the average information content and randomness present in image pixel distributions.

H = - ∑ [ p(i) * log2(p(i)) ]

Where p(i) represents the probability of pixel intensity gray level i appearing in the normalized histogram.

4. ICI Mathematical Fusion Model

Features are mapped into normalized bounds [0, 100] via non-linear logarithmic scaling and integrated using a linear weighted sum model:

ICI = w1*S_edge + w2*S_contour + w3*S_contrast + w4*S_glcm_ent + w5*S_entropy + w6*S_var

Sum of weights Σ w_k = 1.0. Final ICI is mapped to 5 complexity levels (Very Low, Low, Medium, High, Very High).
